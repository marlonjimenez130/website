The clock we prune by

Never above nine and three

Picture a clock face around the crown. A palm feeds itself through its green fronds, so the more of the clock you cut, the less it has to live on.

How much to cut

Dead fronds only · a normal visitBrown fronds and spent flower and fruit stalks come off; the green crown stays whole.

Nine to three · the limit, not the aimOn a normal visit only brown fronds and spent flower or fruit stalks come off. A green frond is cut only if it is a hazard or touches the house, and never above the line from nine to three.

The hurricane cut · what we won’t doStripping a palm to a few upright fronds takes away the leaves it feeds on. It can leave the tree weaker, not safer.

A Naples garden has two seasons

Most of the rain falls from mid-May to mid-October. The work changes with it, so the visits do too.

JanFebMarAprMayJunJulAugSepOctNovDec

Wetmid-May to mid-October

  • Dead fronds and spent stalks removed before storm season; we don’t strip palms for storms
  • Hedges kept back from roofs, screens and walkways
  • Drains and low beds watched after heavy rain
  • A clean-up visit after a storm, when it’s safe

Drymid-October to mid-May

  • Palm feeding on a schedule set for your trees
  • Beds weeded, edged and mulched
  • Irrigation looked over, so plants get what they need
  • New planting, while the weather is kind to it
