Polybutylene repiping in Naples.

Repiping for Naples homes with gray polybutylene pipe. We find it, show you where it runs, and replace it with PEX or copper one area at a time, so your house keeps working while we do.

Signs you need it

When to call.

  • Gray, flexible plastic pipe at the water heater, under sinks, or where the line enters the house.
  • Gray pipe along the ceiling joists in the attic.
  • Pinhole leaks that keep coming back in different places.
  • An insurance inspection that flags your plumbing.
  • A home built from the late 1970s to the mid 1990s.

Not every gray pipe is polybutylene. Text us a photo and we'll tell you.

Why it matters

The pipe that fails from the inside.

Polybutylene was used heavily in new homes from 1978 to 1995. Over time, chemicals in treated water can make it brittle, so it can fail without warning, often at a fitting. In 1995, a national class-action settlement put $950 million toward replacing leaking polybutylene systems.

Sources: NBC 6 South Florida, 2023 (the years; others say late 1970s to mid 1990s); Wikipedia, Polybutylene (brittleness); U.S. Court of Appeals, Fifth Circuit (the settlement).

Why your insurer asks.

Florida's state-backed insurer, Citizens, told NBC 6 in 2023 that it does not insure properties with polybutylene piping, and many insurers ask for a '4-point' inspection on older homes, which records the type of plumbing. Ask your agent what your policy needs. We give you photos and a written record of the new pipe.

Source: NBC 6 South Florida, 2023.

Repiping questions

PEX or copper?

PEX is flexible, needs fewer fittings and usually costs less. Copper is the traditional choice and some owners prefer it. Both are a lasting fix; we'll price both.

Can I just fix the one leak?

You can, and sometimes that's right for now. But leaks that keep coming back in new places usually mean the whole system is aging.

Do I need to move out?

[Usually not: the real shop's answer]. We work one area at a time and cover floors and furniture.

Will it help with my insurance?

Replacing polybutylene removes the item many insurers ask about. Your agent can tell you exactly what your policy needs.
