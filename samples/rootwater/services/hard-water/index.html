Signs you need it

When to call.

  • White crust on faucets and shower heads.
  • Spotty glasses out of the dishwasher.
  • Cloudy shower glass that won't come clean.
  • Soap that won't lather.
  • Orange or brown stains (often iron, more common on wells).
  • A rotten-egg smell from the hot tap or a well.

Know your water

How hard is your water?

It depends where it comes from. Collier County's water utility reported hardness of 42 to 94 mg/L in 2024, an average of 67 mg/L. On the report's own scale, that's moderately hard. City of Naples, Marco Island and other utilities publish their own yearly report. Moderately hard water still leaves scale on glass and inside heaters over time. On a private well, it can be much harder, and only a test will tell you.

Sources: Collier County 2024 Water Quality Report (scale: 0–60 soft, 61–120 moderately hard, 121–180 hard, over 180 very hard); every community water system publishes a yearly report (EPA).

On a private well?

The Florida Department of Health recommends testing a private well for bacteria and nitrate at least once a year. That's a safety test, separate from hardness. We test for hardness, iron and smell; your county health department or a certified lab handles the safety test.

Source: Florida Department of Health, private wells.

Three tools, three jobs.

  • Softener

    Removes the minerals that make scale. Protects glass, fixtures and your heater.

  • Whole-home filter

    Changes taste and smell, and catches sediment.

  • Drinking-water filter

    Reverse osmosis under the sink. For the glass you drink.

A softener doesn't make water safe to drink, and a filter doesn't soften it. We match the tool to what bothers you.

Hard water questions

Is Naples water hard?

Collier County's 2024 report calls its water moderately hard: 42 to 94 mg/L, 67 on average. Your own tap depends on your utility, or on your well. A test at your sink answers it.

Will softened water taste salty?

Most people don't notice, but a salt softener adds a little sodium. If you're watching sodium, use potassium instead of salt, or drink from a filtered tap.
